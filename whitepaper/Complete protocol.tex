\section{The complete protocol}
\label{s:complete protocol}

The complete IOPP is the combination of Protocol \ref{prot:main} with the zero-knowledge measures described in Section \ref{s:zk}.

\subsection{On-domain randomization of $w_1$.}

To randomize the values $w_A(\vec\alpha)$, $w_B(\vec\alpha)$ we choose the basefield strategy explained in Section \ref{s:randomizing:w_M}. 
We apply Lemma \ref{lem:zk:basefield:randomization} to the multilinears $w_A$ and $w_B$ separately.
Concretely, we do the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item  
    We select $s =e$ sets 
    \begin{equation}
    \label{e:zk:propagation gates:points}
    \Omega_j = \{\vec x_{j,1}, \ldots, \vec x_{j,e}\}\subset H_m, \quad j=1,\ldots, e,
    \end{equation}
    and with the property required by the lemma. 
\item 
    For each $i=1,\ldots, e$, we allocate  $2e$ circuit variables in the first stage R1CS, and place their propagation gates in $A$ and $B$ at the points of $\Omega_j$. 
    This results in overall $2e^2$ circuit variables, and their values are drawn uniformly from the basefield $\mathbb F_q$.
\end{enumerate}



\noindent
By Lemma \ref{lem:zk:basefield:randomization}, the exceptional set on which all Moore determinants vanish, i.e.
\begin{equation}
\label{e:complete protocol:exceptional set}
E = \bigcap_{i=1}^e \{\vec\alpha\in F^m \::\: 
\det M_{F^2}(eq(\vec x_{i,1},\vec\alpha), \ldots, eq(\vec x_{i,e2},\vec\alpha)) = 0
\},
\end{equation}
is at most of size
\begin{equation}
\label{e:completeness:error}
\varepsilon < s\cdot \frac{m-t}{|F|} + \left(\frac{t}{q-1}\right)^e,
\end{equation}
with $t = \ceil{\log_2 (e)}$.
For perfect zero-knowledge it is crucial to exclude this set of sumcheck randomnesses.

\subsection{Joint mask commitment.}

We let the joint mask collection for WHIR/ZO0K also cover the sparse sumcheck mask for the zero-knowldege zero-check (Section \ref{s:zk:zerocheck}).
% All masks together, the zerocheck masks plus .
Overall, $C_{zk}$ encodes the randomized joint mask vector  
\begin{equation}
\label{e:complete:prot:masks}
\hat{\mathsf m} = \overbrace{h_0}^\text{zero-check} \hspace*{-3mm}\| \underbrace{h_1\|\ldots \|h_r}_\text{WHIR} \| \overbrace{msk_0\| r_0}^\text{for $w_1$, $w_2$} \| \underbrace{msk_1\| \ldots \| msk_r}_\text{WHIR oracles}\| msk_{zk} 
\in F^{m_\text{zk}},
\end{equation}
where all vector elements are drawn uniformly from $F$, except for $msk_0$ and $r_0$, which are sampled from the basefield $\mathbb F_q$.
Recall that the sizes of $msk_0$, $msk_1,\ldots, msk_r$ and $msk_\text{zk}$ are chosen to obfuscate the number of queries of the oracles (in-domain and DEEP).
We define the space of proper joint mask polynomials as
\begin{equation}
\label{e:space:mask:polys}
\mathscr P_\text{zk} =
\left\{
\hat{\mathsf m}(X) \in F[X]^{<m_\text{zk}} \::\:
\begin{matrix}
\text{coeff. vector $\hat{\mathsf m}$  is as in \eqref{e:complete:prot:masks}},
\\
\text{with $msk_0, r_0$ over $\mathbb F_q$}
\end{matrix}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
To slightly optimize the proof size, the joint mask polynomial $\hat{\mathsf m}$ is committed together with the full-entropy mask 
\begin{equation}
\label{e:full:entropy:mask}
\hat M = H_0 \| H_1\| \ldots \|H_r\|R_0\|MSK_0\|\ldots\|MSK_r\|MSK_\text{zk} \sample F^{m_\text{zk}}    
\end{equation}
for the ZO0K final opening proof as interleaved codeword from $C_\text{zk}^2$, but for simplicity of the presentation we assume that it is committed later on, in the subprotocol \ref{prot:zook:final:opening}.




\subsection{The zk-IOPP}

At start, the prover commits to the joint mask $\hat{\mathsf m}$ as codeword $\hat g \in C_{zk}$ and the randomized first stage witness $w_1$ as masked Reed--Solomon codeword $\hat f_1\in C_1^{e_1}$, using $\msk_{0,1}$ from $\hat{\mathsf m}$.
% \ulrich{same notation for interleaving depth as for extension field degree, needs to be changed}
The target of the IOPP is to prove that $\hat f_1$ and $\hat g$ are proximate to a solution of the R1CS, i.e. that they belong to
\begin{equation}
\label{e:zkIOPP:main:R}
\mathcal R_\theta = \left\{
    \begin{matrix}
    \hat f_1\in \left(\mathbb F_q^{D_1}\right)^{e_1}, 
    \\
    \hat g\in F^{D_\text{zk}}
    \end{matrix}
    \::\:
    \begin{matrix}
    \exists 
    \hat p(X) \in \mathscr P_{m_1}(\mathbb F_q), 
    \hat{\mathsf m}(X) \in \mathscr P_\text{zk},
    \\
    d(\hat p, \hat f_1) \leq \theta \:\wedge\: d(\hat{\mathsf m},\hat g)\leq \theta_\text{zk},
    \\\text{and}
    \\
    \text{the coefficients $(c_i)$ of}
    \\
    p(X) = \hat p(X) - \msk_{0,1}(X)
    \\
    \text{satisfy 
    }
    \\
    (c_i)  \in \mathscr R_{main} \cap \mathscr R_{aux},
    \\
    (c_1,\ldots,c_{t_1}) = \vec{in}
    \end{matrix}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Note that $\hat{\mathsf m}(X)\in\mathscr P_\text{zk}$ guarantees that the mask $\msk_{0,1}(X)$ is over the basefield $\mathbb F_q$, and so is the unmasked proximate $p(X)$ in \eqref{e:zkIOPP:main:R}.
Thus the claimed solution is in fact as intended.

The protocol consists of the following four phases, each consisting of one or several rounds which gradually reduce the main claim that $(\hat f_1, \hat g)\in \mathscr R_\theta$ to simpler claims on the prover messages.

\subsubsection{Phase 0.}
This is the mask binding phase.
It narrows down the prover to a single proximate of its mask commitment, and further ensures that it belongs to $\mathscr P_\text{zk}$.
The verifier sends a random $z\sample F$, and the prover reveals the value $v = \hat{\mathsf m}(z)$, as well as of its randomized component $V_z(\hat{\mathsf m}) = \msk_0(z) + z^{|\msk_0|}\cdot r_0(z)$.
Except with a probability of at most
\begin{equation}
\label{e:soundness:error:distinguishing}
\varepsilon = \binom{\ell(\theta_\text{zk})}{2} \cdot \frac{m_\text{zk} - 1}{|F|},
\end{equation}
the random point $z\sample F$ belongs to the set of distinguishers 
\begin{equation}
\label{e:S}
S = \left\{ z\in F 
\::\: 
\begin{matrix}
\forall \hat q_1(X),\hat q_2(X)\in \mathcal L(\theta_\text{zk}),
%F[X]^{<q_\text{zk}},
\:
q_1(X) \neq q_2(X),
% \\
% d(\hat q_i, g)\leq \theta_\text{zk}, \: i=1,2,
\\
\text{we have}
\\
\hat q_1(z)\neq \hat q_2(z) 
\end{matrix}
%\in \mathcal L(\theta_\text{zk})
\right\},
\end{equation}
% \[
% S = \left\{ z\in F 
% \::\: 
% \begin{matrix}
% E_z: \mathcal L(\theta_\text{zk}) \longrightarrow F, \: \hat q(X) \mapsto q(z)
% \\
% \text{is injective}
% \end{matrix}
% %\in \mathcal L(\theta_\text{zk})
% \right\},
% \]
where 
\[
\mathcal L(\theta_\text{zk}) = 
\big\{\hat q(X) \in F[X]^{<q_\text{zk}} \::\: d(\hat q, \hat g) \leq \theta_\text{zk}
\big\}
\]
is the set of $\theta_\text{zk}$-proximates of $\hat g$, and consequently the condition $\hat q(z) = v$ singles out a single proximate from $\mathcal L(\theta_\text{zk})$.
The value $v' = V_z(\hat{\mathsf m})$ is used to demand that also $v' = V_{\phi(z)}(\hat{\mathsf m})$, where $\phi(z)$ is the Frobenius conjugate of $z$, which overwhelmingly assures that the mask component $\msk_0$ is over the basefield $\mathbb F_q$.
Overall the phase is a randomized reduction 
\[
\mathcal R_{\vec\theta} \stackrel{z}{\longrightarrow} \mathcal R_\text{phase 0},
\]
with
\begin{equation}
\label{e:zkIOPP:R:phase:0}
\mathcal R_\text{phase 0} = \left\{
    \begin{matrix}
    \hat f_1\in \left(\mathbb F_q^{D_1}\right)^{e_1}, 
    \\
    \hat g \in \mathbb F^{D_\text{zk}},
    \\
    v, v' \in F
    \end{matrix}
    \::\:
    \begin{matrix}
    z\in S, 
    \\
    \exists 
    \hat p_1(X) \in \mathscr P_{m_1}, 
    \hat q(X) \in F[X]^{< m_\text{zk}}\big|_{z,v,v'},
    \\
    d(\hat p_1, f) \leq \theta \:\wedge\: d(\hat q,g)\leq \theta_\text{zk},
    \\\text{and}
    \\
    \text{the coefficients $(c_i)$ of}
    \\
    p_1(X) = \hat p_1(X) - \msk_{0,1}(X)
    \\
    \text{satisfy 
    }
    \\
    (c_i)  \in \mathscr R_{main} \cap \mathscr R_{aux},
    \\
    (c_1,\ldots,c_{t_1}) = \vec{in}
    \end{matrix}
\right\},
\end{equation}
where 
\begin{equation}
\label{e:mask:poly:space:punctured}
F[X]^{<m_\text{zk}}\big|_{z,v,v'} = \left\{
\hat q(X)\in F[X]^{<m_\text{zk}} \::\:
\begin{matrix}
\hat q(z) = v, \\
V_z(\hat q) = v', V_{\phi(z)}(\hat q) = \phi(v')
\end{matrix}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
The requirement that $z\in S$ in \eqref{e:zkIOPP:R:phase:0} assures that there is at most one proximate mask satisfying the conditions from \eqref{e:mask:poly:space:punctured}, and will be kept throught the following steps. 
It allows to maintain the same soundness errors of the subsequent phases as in Theorem \ref{thm:main:soundness:informal}.

The R1CS second stage, Phase \ref{i:main:phase:1} in Protocol \ref{prot:main}. 
The only difference is that the second-stage witness $w_2$ is committed as masked codeword $\hat f_2\in C^{e}$, using $\msk_{0,2}$ from $\hat{\mathsf m}$.
The phase is a reduction
\[
\mathcal R_\text{phase 0} \stackrel{\vec\xi}{\longrightarrow} \mathcal R_\text{phase 1}
\]
with %$R_\text{phase 1}$ also incooporating the assumption that $z\in S$.
\begin{equation}
\label{e:}
\mathcal R_\text{phase 1} 
=
\left\{
    \begin{matrix}
        \hat f_1 \in \left(\mathbb F_q^{D_1}\right)^{e_1},
        \\
        \hat g \in \mathbb F^{D_\text{zk}},
        \\
        v, v' \in F
        \\
        \hat f_2^* \in \left(\mathbb F_q^{D_1}\right)^{e_1}%, v_2\in F 
    \end{matrix}
    \::\:
    % \hspace*{-0.25cm}
    \begin{matrix}
        z\in S,
        \\
        \exists (\hat p_1(X),\hat p_2(X)) \in \mathscr P_{m_1}^2,

        \\
        d((\hat p_1, \hat p_2), (\hat f_1,\hat f_2^*)) \leq \theta_1
        \:\wedge \: 
        d(\hat q, \hat g)\leq \theta_\text{zk},
        \\
        \text{and}
        \\
        \text{the coefficient vectors of }
        \\

\end{equation}



\subsubsection{Phase 2.}
This is zero-check, Phase \ref{i:main:phase:2} from Protocol \ref{prot:main}, modified by the zero-knowledge measures described in Section \ref{s:zk:zerocheck}, using the sparse mask $h_0$ from $\hat{\mathsf m}$.
This phase reduces constraint satisfaction of the unmasked proximates $p_1(X)$, $p_2(X)$ to that their combined image polynomials $w_A$, $w_B$, $w_C$ satisfy the linear claims 
\begin{align}
\label{e:zkIOPP:phase3:claim:vM}
v_M = \sum_{\vec x\in H_{m_1}} M_1(\vec\alpha, \vec x) \cdot p_1(\vec x) + \sum_{\vec x\in H_{m_1}} M_2^*(\vec\alpha,\vec x)\cdot p_2(\vec x)
\end{align}
with $M = A,B,C$, and
\begin{align}
\label{e:zkIOPP:phase3:claim:inputs}
in_i = \sum_{\vec x \in H_{m_1}} eq(i, \vec x) \cdot p_1(\vec x),
\quad 
\xi_i = \sum_{\vec x \in H_{m_2}} eq^*(i, \vec x) \cdot p_2(\vec x),
\end{align}
given that all sumcheck consistency checks hold.
Recall that $w_1$ is randomized, and so are the values $v_A$, $v_B$ and $v_C$, as long as the vector of sumcheck randomnesses $\vec\alpha$ is not contained in the exceptional set $E$ as in \eqref{e:complete protocol:exceptional set}. 
Thus if all Moore determinants
\[
\det M(eq(\vec x_{i,1},\vec\alpha), \ldots, eq(\vec x_{i,2e},\vec\alpha) = 0,
\]
then the prover aborts. 
%and reruns the protocol from the start.

Formally, this phase is a randomized reduction 
\[
\mathcal R_\text{phase 1} \stackrel{\vec\rho, \alpha_0,\vec\alpha}{\longrightarrow} \mathcal R_\text{phase 2},
\]
where $\alpha_0$ denotes the randomness for combining the sumcheck mask, and
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_\text{phase 2} =
\left\{
    \begin{matrix}
        \hat f_1 \in \left(\mathbb F_q^{D_1}\right)^{e_1},
        \\
        \hat g \in \mathbb F^{D_\text{zk}},
        \\
        v, v' \in F
        \\
        \hat f_2^* \in \left(\mathbb F_q^{D_1}\right)^{e_1},
        \\
        q_1(X), \ldots, q_m(X) \in F[X]^{\leq 3}
        \\
        \hat v_A, \hat v_B, \hat v_C \in F
    \end{matrix}
    \::\:
    \hspace*{-10mm}
    \begin{matrix}
        z\in S,
        \\
        \exists (\hat p_1(X),\hat p_2(X)) \in \mathscr P_{m_1}^2, 
        \hat q(X) \in F[X]^{< m_\text{zk}}\big|_{z,v,v'},
        \\
        d((\hat p_1, \hat p_2), (\hat f_1,\hat f_2^*)) \leq \theta_1
        \:\wedge \: 
        d(\hat q, \hat g)\leq \theta_\text{zk},
        \\
        \text{and}
        \\
        \sum h_0(\vec x) = q_1(0) + q_1(1),
        \\
        q_i(\alpha_i) = q_{i+1}(0) + q_{i+1}(1),
        \\
        \text{for }i = 1,\ldots, m-1,
        \\
        q_m(\alpha_m) = (\hat v_A \cdot \hat v_B - \hat v_C)\cdot eq(\vec\alpha, \vec\rho),
        \\
        \text{and}
        \\
        p_1(X) = \hat p_1(X) - \msk_{0,1}(X), 
        \\ 
        p_2(X) = \hat p_2(X) - \msk_{0,2}(X),
        \\
        \text{satisfy the linear claims \eqref{e:zkIOPP:phase3:claim:vM} and \eqref{e:zkIOPP:phase3:claim:inputs}}
    \end{matrix}
\right\}.
\end{equation}





\subsubsection{Phase 3: ZO0K.}
We run ZO0K as described in Section \ref{s:zook} on the joint claims \eqref{e:zkIOPP:phase3:claim:vM} and \eqref{e:zkIOPP:phase3:claim:inputs} for the combined polynomial $p(X)$ and $\hat{\mathsf m}$.
To do so, the five claims are combined into one by another randomness.
This phase is a reduction 
\[
\mathcal R_{phase 2} \stackrel{\vec\beta, \ldots}{\longrightarrow} \mathcal V,
\]
where $\mathcal V$ is the final verifier relation, which we defer to Appendix \ref{s:IOPPsoundness:complete}. 
It includes the consistency checks of each ZO0K round, including the those of the final opening proof, and last but not least the final checks on the matrix polynomial specializations $M(\vec\alpha, \vec\beta,\:.\:)$, $M=A,B,C$.


\subsubsection{Protocol summary.}
We summarize the protocol, and highlight the differences to Protocol \ref{prot:main}.

\begin{protocol}[zk-IOPP]
\label{prot:complete}
The R1CS matrices $A_i,B_i,C_i \in \mathbb F_q^{m\times m_i}$, $i=1,2$, and public inputs $\vec{in} = (in_0,\ldots, in_{t})\in \mathbb F_q^t$ are known to both the prover and the verifier. 
The prover has committed to \textcolor{PineGreen}{the joint mask polynomial $\hat{\mathsf m}$ from \eqref{e:complete:prot:masks} as codeword $\hat g\in C_\text{zk}$}, and to the \textcolor{PineGreen}{randomized} first-stage witness $\vec w_1\in \mathbb F_q^{m_1}$ \textcolor{PineGreen}{(cf. Section \ref{s:randomizing:w_M} and Remark \ref{rem:propagation:constraints}} as \textcolor{PineGreen}{masked} Reed--Solomon codeword $\hat f_1\in C^e$. 
The protocol consists of the following four phases.
\begin{enumerate}
\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\textcolor{PineGreen}{
\item 
Mask binding: 
\label{i:zk:phase:0}
The verifier samples 
\[
z\sample F
\]
at random, and the prover answers with $v = \hat{\mathsf m}(z)$, $v' = V_z(\hat{\mathsf m})$ where $V_z$ is the above defined evaluation functional on the mask component $\hat\msk_0 = \msk_0\|r_0$.
}

\item
\label{i:zk:phase:1}
R1CS second stage:
The verifier samples the stage-2 challenge
\[
\vec\xi \sample \mathbb F_q^{t}
\]
and sends them to the prover.
The prover computes $\vec w_2 \in \mathbb F_q^{m_2}$  the second-stage witness of the R1CS, commits to it as \textcolor{PineGreen}{masked} codeword $\hat f_2\in C^e$.

\item 
\label{i:zk:phase:2}
R1CS zero-check:
The verifier sends a random 
\[
\vec\rho \sample F^m,
\]
and both prover and verifier engage in the \textcolor{PineGreen}{zk-sumcheck protocol as described in Section \ref{s:zk:zerocheck}} for 
\begin{align}
\label{e:R1CS:zerocheck}
\sum_{\vec x\in H_m} \left(w_A(\vec x)\cdot w_B(\vec x) - w_C(\vec x)\right)\cdot eq(\vec x, \vec\rho) = 0,
\end{align}
where $w_A(\vec x), w_B(\vec x), w_C(\vec x)$ are the multilinears as specified in \eqref{e:a:poly}, using the unmasked combined witness polynomial $w$. 
After the last round of the sumcheck, \textcolor{PineGreen}{the prover checks if the vector of verifier challenges $\vec\alpha\in F^m$ drawn in the course of the sumcheck, satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{e:alph:notin:E}
\alpha \notin E,
\end{equation}
with $E$ as in  \eqref{e:complete protocol:exceptional set}.}
If this is the case the prover sends 
% (The protocol reduces the claim on the hypercube sum \eqref{e:R1CS:zerocheck} to a quadratic check on the evaluation claims 
\begin{equation*}
v_A = w_A(\vec\alpha),  \quad v_B = w_B(\vec\alpha), \quad v_C = w_C(\vec\alpha).
\end{equation*}
\textcolor{PineGreen}{(Otherwise, it aborts.)}
The verifier checks if $q_m(\alpha_m) = (v_A\cdot v_B - v_C) \cdot eq(\alpha,\rho)$.

\item 
\label{i:main:phase:3}
ZO0K: 
% The verifier samples a random $\lambda\sample F$, 
Both prover and verifier run \textcolor{PineGreen}{ZO0K as in Section \ref{s:zook}} for the following linear constraints on the combined \textcolor{PineGreen}{masked} witness polynomial $\hat w$ \textcolor{PineGreen}{and $\hat{\mathsf m}$}:
\begin{align}
\label{e:phase3:claim:vM}
v_M = \sum_{\vec x\in H_{m_1}} M_1(\vec\alpha, \vec x) \cdot w_1(\vec x) + \sum_{\vec x\in H_{m_1}} M_2^*(\vec\alpha,\vec x)\cdot w_2^*(\vec x)
\end{align}
with $M = A,B,C$, and
\begin{align}
\label{e:phase3:claim:inputs}
in_i = \sum_{\vec x \in H_{m_1}} eq(i, \vec x) \cdot w_1(\vec x),
\quad 
\xi_i = \sum_{\vec x \in H_{m_2}} eq^*(i, \vec x) \cdot w_2^*(\vec x),
\end{align}
for $i = 0,\ldots, t$, \textcolor{PineGreen}{where $w_i = \hat w_i - \msk_{0,i}$, $i=1,2$, 
with the additional mask constraints $\hat{\mathsf m}(z) = 0$, $V_z(\hat{\mathsf m}) = v'$, $V_{\phi(z)}(\hat{\mathsf m}) = \phi(v')$ taken into account in the final opening proof Protocol \ref{prot:zook:final:opening}}.
% (This phase reduces the validity of the linear constraints to claims on the combined matrix polynomials
% \begin{equation}
% \label{e:split:matrix:values}
% A(\vec\alpha,\vec\beta), 
% \quad 
% B(\vec\alpha,\vec\beta), 
% \quad 
% C(\vec\alpha,\vec\beta),
% \end{equation}
% where $\vec\beta \in F^{m_1}$ are the folding randomnesses drawn in the course of WHIR.)
\end{enumerate}
If all verifier checks in Phase \ref{i:main:phase:2} and Phase \ref{i:main:phase:3}, including that of the final opening Protocol \ref{prot:zook:final:opening}, pass, then the verifier accepts.
Otherwise, it rejects.
\end{protocol}

\begin{rem}
Filtering out the few exceptional sumcheck randomnesses $\vec\alpha$ hurts completeness in a negligible manner, with a failure probability of at most \eqref{e:completeness:error}.
To patch completeness, the prover can rerun the protocol and receive a fresh $\vec\alpha$.
\end{rem}



\subsection{Soundness}
\label{s:zkIOPP:soundness:discussion}

Let us summarize the soundness properties of our zk-IOPP as chain of phase-wise interactive oracle reductions
\[
\mathcal R \longrightarrow \mathcal R_\text{phase 0} \longrightarrow \ldots \mathcal R_\text{phase 2} \longrightarrow \mathcal V,
\]
without going into details of their rounds. 
% and likewise for each of the rounds within ZO0K, Phase 3.

We again assume the proximity parameters $\theta_1,\ldots, \theta_r$ for the basecodes $C_1$,\ldots, $C_r$ in the Johnson regime, i.e. below the 
\begin{equation}
\label{e:theta:i:below:Johnson}
\theta_i \in (0,  1 - \sqrt{1 -\delta_i}),
\end{equation}
where $\delta_i$ is the minimum distance of $C_i$, and $\ell_i(\theta_i)$ denote their corresponding list size bounds. 
The same assumption is taken for $\theta_\text{zk}$ the proximity parameter imposed on $C_\text{zk}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{e:theta:zk:below:Johnson}
\theta_\text{zk} \in (0, 1 - \sqrt{1 -\delta_\text{zk}}), 
\end{equation}
where $\delta_\text{zk}$ is the minimum distance of $C_\text{zk}$, and $\ell_\text{zk}(\theta_\text{zk})$ will denote the list size bound. 


\begin{thm}[IOPP soundness]
\label{thm:zk:soundness:informal}
For proximity parameters $\theta_1,\ldots, \theta_r$ and $\theta_\text{zk}$ as above.
Protocol \ref{prot:complete} is a round-by-round knowledge sound interactive oracle proof for that $(\hat f_1, \hat g)\in \mathcal R_{\theta_1}$ as specified in \eqref{e:zkIOPP:main:R}, with the following round-by-round soundness errors for the four phases:
\begin{align}
\label{e:zkIOPP:phase0:soundness:error}
\varepsilon_\text{phase 0} 
&< \frac{\ell_\text{zk}(\theta_\text{zk})\cdot (\ell_\text{zk}(\theta_\text{zk}) + 1)}{2} \cdot \frac{m_\text{zk}}{|F|},
\\
\label{e:zkIOPP:phase1:soundness:error}
\varepsilon_\text{phase 1} 
&\leq 
    % % \max\left( 
    % \binom{\ell_ 1}{2} \cdot \frac{2^{m_1}}{|F|} %\quad
    % + \ell_2\cdot \varepsilon_{stage 2} %\quad
    % + \binom{\ell_2}{2} \cdot \frac{2^{m_2}}{|F|},
    % % \max\right)
    \ell_1(\theta_1) \cdot \varepsilon_\text{R1CS} 
\\\intertext{%
where $\varepsilon_{R1CS}$ is the soundness error of the randomized AIR, 
}\label{e:zkIOPP:phase2:soundness:error}
\varepsilon_\text{phase 2}  
&\leq \ell_1(\theta_1) \cdot \max\left\{\frac{m}{|F|}, \frac{1}{|F|}, \frac{3}{|F|}\right\},
%\cdot \frac{|F|}{|F\setminus E|}, 
\\\intertext{and}
\label{e:zkIOPP:phase3:soundness:error}
\varepsilon_\text{phase 3} &\leq 
\varepsilon_\text{ZO0K}(\theta_1,\ldots, \theta_r,\theta_\text{zk})
\end{align}
for the round-by-round soundness error of ZO0K with $r$ rounds on the inner product claims \eqref{e:phase3:claim:vM} and \eqref{e:phase3:claim:inputs}.
\end{thm}


\begin{rem}
The soundness error \eqref{e:zkIOPP:phase0:soundness:error} of Phase \ref{i:zk:phase:0} follows from the union bound  
\begin{align*}
\frac{\ell(\theta_\text{zk})\cdot (\ell(\theta_\text{zk})-1)}{2} \cdot \frac{m_\text{zk}}{|F|} + \ell(\theta_\text{zk})\cdot \frac{m_\text{zk}}{|F|},    
\end{align*}
where the first term is the probability for that $z\notin S$, and second term is a generous upper bound for the error of the Frobenius check imposed on the component $\hat\msk_0$.
The soundness error \eqref{e:zkIOPP:phase2:soundness:error} of Phase \ref{i:zk:phase:2} now also covers the batching step with the sparse mask polynomial, which contributes to the additional $1/|F|$ term in the max.
\end{rem}

For a proof of Theorem \ref{thm:zk:soundness:informal}, which specifies the reductions of all intermediate steps of each phase, see Appendix \ref{s:IOPPsoundness}.
Since Phase \ref{i:zk:phase:0} binds the prover to a single proximate of the mask commitment, reflected by the sets $\mathcal R_\text{phase 0}$, $\mathcal R_\text{phase 1}$, $\mathcal R_\text{phase 2}$, and $\mathcal V$ incorporating $z\in S$, the soundness error of the PIOP is scaled only by
\[
\ell_1(\theta_1) \cdot 1,
\]
instead of the combinatorial bound 
\[
\ell_1(\theta_1)\cdot \ell_\text{zk}(\theta_\text{zk}),
\]
when omitting Phase \ref{i:zk:phase:0}.
The same principle applies to the roundwise errors of ZO0K, which we summarize now.

\begin{thm}[ZO0K soundness under mask binding]
\label{thm:zook:soundness}
Assume that $z\in S$.
Then round $i=1,\ldots, r$ in Protocol \ref{prot:WHIR} with the modifications of Section \ref{s:zook}
is round-by-round (knowledge) sound oracle reduction with the following knowledge soundness errors for each of its steps.

(There, $\varepsilon_\text{CA}(C,d,\theta)$ denotes the soundness error of the constrained correlated agreement theorem (Theorem \ref{thm:CAT:constrained}) for the Reed--Solomon code $C$, under degree-$d$ constraints, with proximity parameter $\theta$.)
\begin{enumerate}
    \item 
    Sumcheck phase:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item 
        The step of combining the sparse sumcheck mask has error
        \begin{align}
        \label{e:zook:soundness:batching}
        \varepsilon_{B,i} &= \varepsilon_{\text{CA}}(C_{i},1,\theta_{i}).
        \end{align}
        \item 
        For each of the $r_i$ sumcheck steps,
        \begin{align}
        \label{e:zook:soundness:sumcheck}
           \varepsilon_{S,i} &= \varepsilon_{\text{CA}}(C_{i},2,\theta_{i}).
        \end{align}
    \end{enumerate}
    \item 
    Query phase:
    \begin{align}
    \label{e:zook:soundness:query}
        \varepsilon_{Q,i} = \frac{\ell_{i+1}(\theta_{i+1})^2}{2} \cdot \frac{2^{k_{i+1}}}{|F\setminus D_{i+1}|} + (1 -\theta_{i})^{s_i},
    \end{align}
    where $\ell_{i+1}(\theta_{i+1})$ is the list size bound for $C_{i+1}$ at distance $\theta_{i+1}$.
    (In the last round $i=r$, no code switch is done, and $\ell_{r+1}(\theta_{r+1})$ above is replaced by $\ell_r(\theta_r)$.) 
    \item 
    Aggregation step:
    \begin{align}
    \label{e:zook:soundness:aggregation}
        \varepsilon_{A,i} = \ell_{i+1}(\theta_{i+1}) \cdot \frac{s_{i}}{|F|},
    \end{align}
    again with the same list size bound as above.
    (In the last round $i=r$, $\ell_{r+1}(\theta_{i+1})$ above is replaced by $\ell_r(\theta_r)$.) 
\end{enumerate}
The roundwise soundness errors of the final opening proof, Protocol \ref{prot:zook:final:opening}, are
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{fin,B} = \max\big\{
\varepsilon_{CA}(C_r,1,\theta_r), \varepsilon_{CA}(C_\text{zk},1,\theta_\text{zk})
\big\}
\end{equation}
for the batching step, and 
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{fin,Q} = (1 - \theta_r)^{s} + (1 - \theta_\text{zk})^{s_\text{zk}}
\end{equation}
for the query step.
\end{thm}

For a proof of the theorem, including the specifications of the oracle reductions, we refer to Appendix \ref{s:IOPPsoundness:zook}.



\subsection{Zero-knowledge}

Finally, and without specifying the formal notions, we summarize the zero-knowledge property of our zk-IOPP.

\begin{thm}[IOPP perfect zero-knowledge]
\label{thm:IOPPzk:complete:informal}
Under the assumption that the masks are sampled as described above, and that $w_1$ incooporates the $e^2$ random field elements as described in Section \ref{s:randomizing:w_M}, 
Protocol \ref{prot:complete} is perfect zero-knowledge. 
\end{thm}

The theorem follows from the corresponding properties of the zk-sumcheck (Section \ref{s:zk:zerocheck}) applied to the zero-check and the sumcheck rounds of ZO0K, the sizes of the random masks $\msk_0,\ldots, \msk_r$, and the zero-knowledge property of the final opening proof.
For the detailed statement plus its proof we again refer to Appendix \ref{s:IOPPzk}.
