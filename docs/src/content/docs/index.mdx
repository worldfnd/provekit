## What ProveKit gives you

| Capability | What it means in practice |
| :--- | :--- |
| Noir-to-R1CS compilation | Author circuits in Noir, then let ProveKit prepare prover and verifier artifacts from the compiled circuit. |
| WHIR proof generation | Generate proofs from prepared key material and circuit inputs. |
| Multi-host verification | Verify locally, in Rust services, in WebAssembly, through FFI/mobile hosts, or through the verifier server. |
| Recursive-verifier bridge | Export `params_for_recursive_verifier` and `r1cs.json` for the Go/gnark wrapper when a Groth16 outer proof is required. |
| Artifact-first operations | Treat `.pkp`, `.pkv`, `proof.np`, recursive params, and R1CS exports as versioned deployment artifacts with provenance. |

## Artifact rules

<div class="pk-metric-grid">
  <div class="pk-metric"><strong>.pkp</strong><span>Prover-side key produced by `prepare` and consumed by `prove`.</span></div>
  <div class="pk-metric"><strong>.pkv</strong><span>Verifier-side key produced by `prepare` and consumed by `verify` and recursive export.</span></div>
  <div class="pk-metric"><strong>proof.np</strong><span>Proof produced by `prove` and consumed by verification paths.</span></div>
  <div class="pk-metric"><strong>r1cs.json</strong><span>Recursive-verifier R1CS export produced from a matching verifier/proof pair.</span></div>
</div>

Do not mix artifacts generated from different circuits, commits, branches, compiler backends, hash choices, or proof/verifier pairs.
